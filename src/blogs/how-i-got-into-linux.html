How I Got Into Linux

Home| About| Blog| Interests| Tools| Doing| Git

2026-10-03

It started around the end of 2019, when I got my first computer, a low-end PC. My mom had been against the idea. Then one day she told me, "You should get a computer, it might help you do this or that." I was shocked.

It turned out my maternal uncle and my grandma had convinced her. My uncle already worked in tech, and I kept ending up on my grandma's computer, messing around with basic HTML and CSS. I don't know what he saw in me, but it worked.

Before that I'd always dreamed of owning a little Raspberry Pi, a pocket computer I could make my own. In Nepal it cost around Rs 5,000 before tax. So I looked at the second-hand market and found out I could assemble a PC from used parts. On Hamrobazar, which is basically Nepal's eBay, I got an old CRT monitor for around Rs 2,000 and a CPU tower for around Rs 3,000. It had a working Core 2 Duo, 4 GB of DDR2 RAM and two hard drives, with Windows 7 already installed. A random keyboard was Rs 200 and the mouse was free.

I barely knew anything about computers. I watched videos by a penetration tester on YouTube. They helped me understand computers in theory, but I had almost no hands-on experience. I clicked random things and followed the good old "mess around and find out" approach. I knew there was a power button, a login screen and a Start menu, and that was about it.

As time passed the PC felt painfully slow. I searched for fixes online, and most of them were random registry tweaks, which I applied without understanding them. Windows 7 kept struggling.

Then that same penetration tester said the word that got stuck in my head: Linux. Light, fast, good for old machines. He'd mentioned Kali and Parrot before, but I assumed they were for professional penetration testers and that I'd need some training before using them.

Meanwhile my grandma's laptop looked completely different from my Windows machine, even the logo. I googled everything I noticed and found out it was running Linux Mint. I'd already been doing HTML and CSS on a Linux machine without realizing it.

So I watched a YouTube tutorial and installed Mint myself. I was terrified of messing up my PC. But I knew a motherboard can have several SATA ports, so I looked inside, and there was an extra hard drive that wasn't even connected. It felt like winning the lottery. I installed Mint on it, and from there it was trial and error: the terminal, installing packages, editing config files, a bit like how BIGSWE explores things in his videos.

I moved to Ubuntu, but it was too heavy for my machine. Then came Pop!_OS, and I started watching Derek Taylor (DistroTube), Luke Smith, Kris Occhipinti and Alexey Kutepov. That led to the distro-hopping: Debian minimal, Arch, Artix, Fedora, Void, and finally NixOS. And here I am, happily on NixOS.
