Linux

It started with a second-hand PC that was painfully slow on Windows 7, and a spare hard drive I was terrified to touch. Several distros later, I'm on NixOS. Read the full story...

Why NixOS

I wanted to experiment with a declarative system. I was already using the Nix package manager on Fedora, with home-manager set up there. When I had to replace my hard drive, I realized I'd have to install and configure everything again. I wanted a system where I could reproduce my setup without rebuilding it by hand.

With Nix and home-manager I can reuse my config on any distro that supports Nix. It's pretty chill once you get past the learning curve.

Why Emacs

Because I heard it could be everything. I was already using Vim and Neovim, and Reddit was full of users of both arguing about which one was better. Vim and Neovim users loved to troll Emacs users, which I found entertaining. Plenty of the people I follow use Emacs as their daily driver.

What sold me was that it could be much more than a text editor: a news reader, music player, browser, file manager, image viewer, Org, Magit, and whatever else I could throw at it.

Web dev

I started with basic HTML and CSS on my grandma's computer and I've kept at it since. It's an unfashionable niche among today's programmers, but I like it. TypeScript is the latest thing I picked up.

Chess

I honestly don't know why I started. I kept watching Hikaru and GothamChess on YouTube while barely knowing more than how the pieces move. One day I made a chess.com account and found out I was around 600 Elo. I'm still pretty bad at it, and I still don't know why I started.

Books

I like books with big fonts, a logical or philosophical argument, and a straightforward narrative. I also like having pictures along the way; huge books with tiny print and no images tend to lose me pretty quickly.

Music

R&B, rap, Sherpa Selo and rock, and I vibe with almost every genre. Queen is my favourite band. Bohemian Rhapsody was the first song of theirs I heard, thanks to my brother.

J. Cole and Kanye West are my favourite rappers. I like the idea behind "I am the Kanye," but what interests me even more is how that idea became a phrase. I've heard the line interpreted as "I am the only one." Then Kanye became Ye, which I've heard interpreted as "you" or "us." I don't know if that's what he intended, but the combination stuck with me. It makes me think about how identity can be both intensely personal and shared with other people. I can't quite explain why. His music has the same kick I get from J. Cole.

Football

I started supporting Chelsea around the end of 2018. My friends and I played FPL on our phones and talked mostly about the Premier League. One of them was a die-hard Manchester United fan.

Somehow Eden Hazard kept earning me more than 15 points a game. I also had Salah and Aubameyang in my team, but Hazard had that presence. One day I decided to pick a team. I wanted an English club with a good online community and an underdog feel. Chelsea fit, and Hazard was there. I never looked back.

Tibetan Buddhism and thangkas

As a Sherpa, I've been surrounded by Tibetan Buddhist traditions since childhood. Our villages have big monasteries (gumbas) adorned with beautiful paintings of Buddhas and bodhisattvas. I've always loved the art, even though I can't paint anything like that myself.

Lately, I've also been getting curious about the philosophy behind these traditions, particularly the ideas I grew up around but never really questioned.
